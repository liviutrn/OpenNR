## Full-patch appearance-loss preparation

`vgg_appearance_loss.py` adds optional frozen LPIPS VGG v0.1 supervision at the full 512-pixel training patch resolution, without the current SqueezeNet loss's resize to 256. This is an additional training objective, not a replacement of the pixel/detail loss or an embedded inference backbone. Display RGB is clamped to [0,1] and mapped to [-1,1] as required by LPIPS. The separate AlexNet LPIPS evaluator remains outside the training objective.

Motivation: [Johnson et al.'s perceptual-loss work](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/jcjohns/eccv16/) demonstrates feature-based supervision for feed-forward image transformation, and the [LPIPS authors](https://github.com/richzhang/PerceptualSimilarity) describe VGG as the variant closer to traditional perceptual loss for optimization. This motivates a controlled experiment; it does not prove improvement on Skyrim or solve missing temporal conditioning.

The official torchvision VGG16 checkpoint was downloaded through the installed LPIPS/torchvision implementation. `test_vgg_appearance.py` passed zero identity distance, nonzero finite input gradients, and frozen weights. A real ten-step CUDA smoke using the context model, full-size patches, fresh crops and two workers completed successfully in `vgg_training_smoke`. Its weights are diagnostic only. `train_long_student.py --vgg-weight 0.05` enables the extra loss and records it in the schedule/provenance; resume rejects an objective mismatch. With the default zero weight, prior experiments keep their original objective and checkpoint compatibility.

## Runtime rebuild and FP8 calibration

The balanced best-MAE export was rebuilt through the existing FP16 TensorRT path. TensorRT 10.13.3.9 left/right numerical parity passed with mean differences 6.14e-5 and 5.65e-5 against the PyTorch FP32 reference; the GPU-resident engine measured 12.24 ms per eye and 24.28 ms sequential stereo. The actual D3D12 shared-resource bridge also passed all six input byte checks and exact output bytes, but measured 25.39 ms per stereo pair. This context model is therefore not yet within the 90 Hz frame budget and has not been integrated into Skyrim.

The best-feature export has its own exact FP16 engine for A/B. It passes left/right numerical parity (6.01e-5 and 5.50e-5 mean difference) and the D3D12 bridge checks; its bridge timing is 25.41 ms stereo. The two exports therefore remain quality tradeoffs with equivalent runtime cost, rather than one universal winner.

FP8 was tested as an explicit calibrated deployment experiment. NVIDIA TensorRT requires FP8 conversion to be represented by explicit Q/DQ scales, so the builder flag alone was not used. `tools/quantize_fp8.py` uses NVIDIA Model Optimizer 0.41 with full-eye, training-only calibration rows; no validation/test rows were used. Full Conv+MatMul FP8 with 16 and 64 calibration eyes built, but both failed the numerical gate (left mean error about 0.00238/0.00240) and were slower at about 27.8 ms stereo. Those engines remain preserved as failed experiments, not fallbacks.

A conservative MatMul-only FP8 control inserts eight Q/DQ nodes and passes both stereo numerical checks (6.21e-5 left, 6.30e-5 right mean difference; maxima below 0.0016). Its D3D12 bridge passes the same exact input/output checks and measures 25.22 ms stereo, which is within timing noise of FP16 and supplies no material speed gain. Keep `runtime_context_face_loss_best_mae_fp16/student_fp16.engine` as the known-good runtime fallback; keep the MatMul-only FP8 engine only for further profiling, not as a promoted delivery. The quantized path is an engineering result, not proof of live-game, temporal or headset acceptance.
